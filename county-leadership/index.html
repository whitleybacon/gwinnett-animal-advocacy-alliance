County Leadership: Animal Welfare Actions & Decisions

GAAA tracks significant votes, actions and public statements by Gwinnett County leadership related to animal welfare. This record will be updated as new decisions are made so residents can see not only who represents them, but how County leaders have acted on issues affecting animals in our community.

February 2026

Veterinary Services Contract Approved

Gwinnett County Board of Commissioners • Unanimous Vote

All five members of the Gwinnett County Board of Commissioners approved a new veterinary-services agreement in February 2026. The vote came after GAAA had provided County leadership with its January 2026 Medical Oversight, Accountability & Risk Review, which documented recurring concerns involving medical monitoring and reassessment, medication administration and documentation, post-operative care, delayed diagnosis and escalation, disease prevention and infection control, veterinary coverage, and oversight of contracted veterinary services.

That January review recommended that the County seriously evaluate transitioning to permanent in-house veterinary capacity to improve continuity of care, accountability, and timely access to treatment. Despite those documented concerns, the Board proceeded with the contracted veterinary-services model.

Commissioners Voting to Approve

Nicole Love Hendrickson • Kirkland Carden • Ben Ku • Jasper Watkins III • Matthew Holtkamp

The approval matters as part of the public accountability record because the Board had already received documented concerns about the County's veterinary-care model before voting on the new agreement.

What Happened After Approval

Many of the Same Types of Concerns Were Documented Again

GAAA's subsequent 2026 medical and contract review identified many of the same categories of concern under the new agreement, including gaps in documented daily medical monitoring and follow-up, prescribed medications with no documented doses administered, and deficiencies involving controlled-substance records and other contract requirements.

These recurring findings raise continuing questions about whether Gwinnett County's contracted veterinary-care model provides sufficient continuity, oversight, and accountability — and whether the County is adequately verifying that the veterinary services it contracts and pays for are actually being performed.

August 2026

Animal Advisory Council Restructuring

Gwinnett County Board of Commissioners • Unanimous Vote

All five members of the Gwinnett County Board of Commissioners voted to repeal the existing seven-member Animal Advisory Council and replace it with a five-member, qualification-based structure. The change eliminated guaranteed district-based citizen representation and changed how members and Council leadership are selected.

Public Statement Following the Vote

Commissioner Kirkland Carden — District 1

Following the restructuring, Commissioner Carden publicly defended the decision and accused former Council members of repeatedly “overstepping their boundaries,” “interfering” with County staff and “actively subverting public meeting laws.”

One example he cited involved a Council proposal for additional meetings without shelter staff, which Carden characterized as an attempt to meet in private. GAAA reviewed the former Council bylaws and found no provision requiring shelter staff to attend Council meetings. The bylaws required meetings to remain open to the public and comply with Georgia's Open Meetings Act.

GAAA has asked the County to provide evidence supporting the allegations and to explain why concerns involving individual Council members, if substantiated, required dissolving the entire Council and eliminating district-based citizen representation.

Animal Advisory Council

On August 4, 2026, the Gwinnett County Board of Commissioners repealed the former seven-member Animal Advisory Council structure and replaced it with a new five-member, qualification-based Council.

Because the former Council was dissolved and its members' terms ended, the prior member table has been removed from this page.

What Changed

  • The Council was reduced from seven members to five.
  • Guaranteed representation connected to each commission district was eliminated.
  • Members must now fit specified professional or organizational categories.
  • The Director of Community Services recommends candidates for appointment.
  • The Director of Animal Welfare & Enforcement serves as the Council's non-voting chair.

Why GAAA Is Concerned

The former structure provided direct citizen representation connected to each commission district and allowed the Council to maintain member leadership independent of the department it was charged with advising.

Under the new structure, County administration and Animal Welfare leadership have substantially greater influence over who serves and how the Council is led.

GAAA's Response

GAAA is asking the Board of Commissioners to amend the new ordinance to restore meaningful citizen representation, preserve independent community oversight, and allow voting Council members to elect their own chair and vice-chair.
