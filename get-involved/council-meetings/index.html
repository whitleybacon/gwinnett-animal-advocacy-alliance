Animal Advisory Council Meetings & Updates

Stay informed about changes to the Animal Advisory Council, public meetings, and opportunities for community participation in Gwinnett County.

Important Update: Animal Advisory Council Restructured

On August 4, 2026, the Gwinnett County Board of Commissioners voted to restructure the Animal Advisory Council, ending the terms of all seven existing members.

The former council included citizen representatives appointed by individual commissioners. The replacement council consists of five qualification-based positions, eliminating guaranteed district-based citizen representation.

The decision came after three council members began raising questions about shelter operations, transparency, and accountability. More than 50 residents attended the July 13 Animal Advisory Council meeting.

At the time, the council had also been working on a countywide spay/neuter voucher program and other initiatives intended to address pet overpopulation.

The previously announced September 3 special meeting is no longer listed here as an upcoming meeting.

Residents should consult Gwinnett County's official meeting notices for current information about the restructured council.

What Changed?

The August 4 restructuring changed the council's membership, representation, and leadership structure.

Seven Members Replaced

The former seven-member council was dissolved and replaced with a five-member, qualification-based structure.

District Representation Eliminated

The restructuring removed guaranteed district-based citizen representation from the council.

New Leadership Structure

The Animal Welfare & Enforcement Director serves as the nonvoting chair of the restructured council.

Why Did the County Restructure the Council?

Gwinnett County describes the restructuring as an effort to create a skills-based council with specialized professional expertise to address animal welfare needs.

GAAA has raised concerns about the timing of the decision, the elimination of district-based citizen representation, and the changes to the council's leadership and appointment structure.

GAAA has also requested clarification about the reasons for dissolving the entire existing council rather than addressing specific concerns about its operation.

Why Public Participation Still Matters

The restructuring changed the council's composition, but public participation in animal welfare discussions remains important.

Residents can continue following official meeting announcements, attending public meetings, reviewing agendas and minutes, and contacting elected officials about animal welfare concerns.

Community engagement helps keep issues affecting animals, residents, rescues, volunteers, fosters, adopters, and taxpayers visible.

Meaningful change requires ongoing participation, clear communication, and accountability.
