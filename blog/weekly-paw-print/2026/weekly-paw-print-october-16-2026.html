🐾 The Weekly Paw Print

Your Weekly Look at Gwinnett Animal Welfare | Week Ending October 16, 2026

Accountability, community engagement, and prevention are at the center of this week's animal welfare developments in Gwinnett County.

A new investigation is raising questions about the County's decision to restructure its Animal Advisory Council. Meanwhile, GAAA's state-level advocacy continues to gain momentum, with 12 candidates and legislators now signed on to our Animal Welfare Pledge and an important opportunity for advocates to meet with candidates this Sunday.

We're also highlighting three upcoming opportunities for free preventive veterinary services in Gwinnett—reminders of how important accessible community resources are to addressing animal welfare challenges before they reach our shelters.

Here's what's happening this week.

📰 New Investigation Raises Questions About Gwinnett's Animal Advisory Council Restructuring

More than two months after Gwinnett County commissioners voted on August 4 to dissolve and restructure the Animal Advisory Council, questions remain about the circumstances surrounding that decision.

On October 15, The North Gwinnett Voice published an investigation examining the County's explanations for the restructuring and the information commissioners relied upon before voting to immediately end the terms of all seven sitting Council members.

According to the reporting, the County's original explanation indicated that the restructuring was not prompted by concerns about individual Council members. However, commissioners later referenced allegations involving Council conduct and other concerns when explaining their decisions.

The article also examines the timing of the agenda addition and the process leading up to the unanimous vote.

These questions are especially significant because the Council had been working on animal welfare issues, including a potential countywide spay/neuter voucher program, at a time when Gwinnett continues to face serious pet overpopulation challenges.

Transparency and accountability are essential to public trust. When significant changes are made to a public advisory body, residents deserve clear explanations and an accurate understanding of the information that informed those decisions.

We encourage residents to read the investigation and consider the questions it raises about local government decision-making and public participation.

🏛️ GAAA's State-Level Advocacy Continues to Grow

While accountability at the local level remains important, GAAA is also working to address the broader policies that affect animal welfare throughout Georgia.

We're excited to share that 12 Georgia candidates and legislators have now signed GAAA's Georgia General Assembly Animal Welfare Pledge!

Our 11-point pledge focuses on meaningful, prevention-based solutions, including expanding access to affordable spay/neuter services, strengthening animal cruelty laws and enforcement, addressing irresponsible breeding, improving transparency, and encouraging collaboration between state and local governments.

Each signature represents a public commitment to these priorities. But signing a pledge is only the beginning. Real progress will depend on turning those commitments into legislation, funding, and effective policies that make a difference for animals and communities.

With early voting now underway for the November 3 General Election, we encourage Georgia residents to learn where candidates stand on animal welfare issues.

Animal welfare is not a partisan issue, and GAAA continues reaching out to candidates and elected officials from both parties.

Visit our Election Hub to follow candidate outreach, pledge signers, and our ongoing work at the state level.

🐾 Community Resources: Free Preventive Pet Care in Gwinnett

Advocacy and public policy are important parts of improving animal welfare, but so is making preventive care accessible to the people and animals who need it.

Over the next several days, Gwinnett residents and community cat caregivers have multiple opportunities to access free veterinary services. These programs help protect pets, support responsible caregiving, and, in the case of spay/neuter services, directly reduce unwanted litters.

Gwinnett County's Steve and Julie Helman Pet Vaccine Clinics continue this Saturday at the Bill Atkinson Animal Welfare Center.

Made possible through a donation from Stephen J. Helman's estate, the program provides free vaccinations and microchipping to help Gwinnett residents protect their pets.

Saturday, October 17 | 12–3 PM
Bill Atkinson Animal Welfare Center
884 Winder Highway, Lawrenceville

Free vaccinations and microchipping for dogs and cats.

Preregistration required. Limit of three pets per household. Dogs must be on non-retractable leashes and cats must be in carriers.

We are encouraged to see Gwinnett County investing in accessible preventive services and hope to see similar investments in affordable spay/neuter programs.

🐾 The Paw Print Takeaway

This week's developments highlight several parts of the same larger challenge.

At the local level, transparency and accountability are essential to ensuring that animal welfare decisions serve the community. At the state level, meaningful policy changes require legislators who understand the challenges and residents willing to engage with them.

And on the ground, preventive services—from vaccinations and microchipping to spay/neuter programs—help address animal welfare needs before they become shelter crises.

We cannot solve pet overpopulation by managing the consequences alone. We must address the causes.

GAAA will continue advocating for responsible policies, accessible prevention programs, transparency, accountability, and meaningful collaboration across Georgia.

Because lasting change requires more than recognizing the problem. It requires action.
