Week Ending October 9, 2026

This week brought encouraging developments for animal welfare in Gwinnett and across Georgia. More candidates and legislators signed GAAA's Animal Welfare Pledge, statewide animal welfare advocacy received media attention, and Gwinnett's new free pet vaccine clinics began with a tremendous community response.

Here's what happened this week.

🏛️ GAAA's State-Level Advocacy Gains Momentum

This was an especially encouraging week for GAAA's Georgia General Assembly Animal Welfare Pledge, with seven additional signatures reported to GAAA.

Our newest signers include Rep. Sam Park, Rep. Scott Hilton, Jennifer Ambler, Michelle Kang, Lisa Maria, Jyot Singh, and Maureen Gault.

We're particularly encouraged to see our outreach expanding beyond Gwinnett into neighboring Forsyth County, where Maureen Gault is a candidate.

Our 11-point pledge addresses affordable spay/neuter access, animal cruelty laws and enforcement, responsible breeding, transparency, accountability, and collaboration between state and local governments.

We continue reaching out to candidates and legislators from both parties. Each signature is a public commitment to the priorities in the pledge, but signing is only the beginning.

The real measure of progress will be turning those commitments into legislation, funding, and meaningful action.

📰 Animal Welfare Advocacy Gets Statewide Attention

On October 8, Georgia Recorder published an article highlighting the Georgia Pet Coalition's efforts to educate voters about the animal welfare records of Georgia lawmakers.

The article discusses animal welfare legislation, shelter deaths, and the importance of affordable spay/neuter programs.

Although the article features the Georgia Pet Coalition rather than GAAA, it highlights statewide issues that overlap with the priorities behind our own legislative outreach.

Animal welfare challenges do not stop at county lines, and neither should the search for solutions.

💉 Gwinnett's Free Pet Vaccine Clinics Begin

One of this week's most encouraging local developments was the start of the Steve and Julie Helman Pet Vaccine Clinics at the Bill Atkinson Animal Welfare Center on Wednesday, October 7.

The first clinic drew an overwhelming community response and a huge turnout, showing just how much residents value access to preventive pet care.

Made possible through a donation from Stephen J. Helman's estate and implemented by Gwinnett County, the clinics offer free vaccines for dogs and cats, along with microchipping.

We want to acknowledge this investment in preventive animal welfare services and the strong response from Gwinnett residents.

Upcoming clinic dates:

Saturday, October 17 | 12–3 PM

Wednesday, November 4 | 4–7 PM

Saturday, November 21 | 12–3 PM

Wednesday, December 2 | 4–7 PM

Saturday, December 19 | 12–3 PM

Clinics are for Gwinnett County residents, with a limit of three pets per household. Dogs must be on non-retractable leashes and cats must be in carriers. Preregistration is required.

🐾 The Paw Print Takeaway

This week showed two important forms of progress: more public commitments to animal welfare at the state level and more accessible preventive services for Gwinnett pet owners.

Both deserve recognition. And both are starting points, not the finish line.

GAAA will continue advocating for prevention, accessible spay/neuter, transparency, accountability, and meaningful solutions that address the causes—not simply the consequences—of pet overpopulation.
