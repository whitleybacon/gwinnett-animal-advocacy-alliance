A Commissioner Requested the County's Position in Writing. Where Is It?

Gwinnett Animal Advocacy Alliance | Updated September 20, 2026

For more than a month, Gwinnett Animal Advocacy Alliance (GAAA) has been using Georgia's Open Records Act to seek records surrounding the dismantling of the Animal Advisory Council and other animal welfare decisions.

We have encountered records withheld under attorney-client privilege, responses stating that no additional records exist, and questions about how decisions were made that remain unanswered.

One recent series of records requests concerns a written response that Commissioner Ben Ku's office specifically asked county staff to provide.

Commissioner Ku asked for the County's position in writing. The County has now stated that no responsive documents were found and that no meeting or feedback occurred.

The July 9 Request for the County's Position

GAAA had invited Commissioner Ku to meet with us about animal welfare concerns and our candidate questionnaire.

On July 9, Commissioner Ben Ku's office contacted Animal Welfare & Enforcement Director Katrina Helman, copying Community Services Director Lindsey Jorstad and Ku. His office sought the County's "official position or talking points" regarding animal welfare concerns raised by GAAA.

On July 10, Jorstad offered to meet with the Commissioner to review the concerns.

On July 14, Ku's office responded:

"I spoke with Commissioner Ku, and he would prefer a written response that he can refer to rather than a meeting."

The documented sequence is straightforward. Ku's office requested the County's position, Jorstad offered a meeting, and Ku's office said he preferred a written response instead.

GAAA subsequently submitted an Open Records Request seeking the requested written response and related communications.

What the County Initially Provided

In response to GAAA's August 27 records request, the County provided the same July correspondence that GAAA already possessed.

The County also identified one additional responsive email but withheld it in its entirety, citing attorney-client privilege.

The County then stated:

"No additional records exist responsive to your request."

At that point, we did not know the withheld email's date, sender, recipients, subject line, or relationship to Ku's request.

We could not determine whether it contained the requested written response, related to preparing a response, or served some other purpose.

We Asked for the Email's Identifying Information

GAAA followed up seeking basic identifying information about the withheld email, including:

  • Who sent the email?
  • Who received it?
  • When was it sent?
  • Who was copied?
  • What was its subject line?

In response to an earlier follow-up, the County stated:

"The obligation under the Open Records Act is to provide documents. We are not required to answer questions. This request is complete."

So we submitted another Open Records Request on September 2, specifically seeking existing records or electronic data containing the identifying information.

We did not request the email's body or any privileged legal advice.

The County Released the Header. The Email Was Sent to County Legal.

On September 8, the County responded to that request and produced a redacted email.

The newly released information identifies the message as follows:

  • Date: July 9, 2026
  • Time: 3:34 p.m. Eastern (19:34:48 UTC)
  • From: Lindsey Jorstad, Community Services Director
  • To: Allison Cauthen, Assistant County Attorney
  • Copied: Katrina Helman, Animal Welfare & Enforcement Director
  • Subject: Fw: Candidate Questionnaire & Meeting Invitation for Animal Welfare

The email's contents remain redacted. The County cited attorney-client privilege as the basis for withholding the body.

The released record establishes that Jorstad forwarded correspondence about GAAA's candidate questionnaire and meeting invitation to Assistant County Attorney Allison Cauthen on July 9. It does not reveal what the email said or why she forwarded it.

Why Was This Sent to County Legal?

This is an important question raised by the newly released record.

The subject line connects the forwarded message to GAAA's candidate questionnaire and meeting invitation. Jorstad sent it to Assistant County Attorney Allison Cauthen and copied the Animal Welfare & Enforcement Director.

Because the body is redacted, we do not know what Jorstad communicated, whether she requested legal advice, or what prompted her to forward the correspondence.

Contacting County Legal is not inherently improper. But the public record does not explain why County Legal was brought into this particular exchange or what role, if any, that involvement played in the handling of Ku's inquiry.

The timing also changes our understanding of the withheld email.

The forwarded email was sent on July 9, five days before Ku's office stated on July 14 that he preferred a written response rather than a meeting.

It therefore cannot itself have been a response to that later July 14 preference.

Ku's office had also made an initial request for the County's position on July 9. We have not established whether Jorstad's 3:34 p.m. email preceded or followed that initial request.

The email's contents and its precise purpose remain unknown.

A Separate Request Asked What Happened to the Written Response

GAAA submitted another targeted Open Records Request on September 9.

This request sought existing records documenting the preparation, transmission, delivery, or receipt of the written response Commissioner Ku requested.

We asked for any responsive written response, talking points, emails, memoranda, briefing materials, or communications showing that material was provided to Ku or his staff.

We also requested any existing record showing that the response was not completed, was not provided, or that no further response would be provided.

The County responded the same day:

"Gwinnett County has reviewed its files and has determined there are no responsive documents to your request. No meeting was held nor feedback provided."
The County's September 9 response reports that no responsive documents were located and states that no meeting or feedback occurred.

This is the clearest response GAAA has received about what happened after Ku's office requested the County's position in writing.

We are reporting the County's statement as its response to our records request. We cannot independently establish from that statement alone that no other communication occurred.

What We Now Know — and What We Don't

The new records resolve several questions that were open when this article was first published.

We now know the identity, date, recipients, and subject line of the email previously withheld in its entirety.

We know it was sent by Jorstad to Assistant County Attorney Cauthen on July 9, before Ku's office expressed its preference for a written response on July 14.

And we know that, when asked specifically for records concerning the preparation or delivery of that written response, the County reported finding none and stated that no meeting or feedback occurred.

What remains unknown is why Jorstad forwarded the earlier correspondence to County Legal and what the redacted message contained.

The release of the header shows that identifying information was provided separately from the redacted email contents. It does not establish that the remaining contents, or other withheld records, must legally be disclosed.

This Is Not an Isolated Records Concern

GAAA has encountered similar withholding issues in other records requests.

When we sought records to determine who initiated the Animal Advisory Council restructuring and when, the County acknowledged that responsive records existed but withheld them under attorney-client privilege.

Another resident independently sought records concerning the restructuring and encountered additional records withheld under attorney-client privilege and attorney work product.

These are separate requests involving different records. None of these withholding decisions, by itself, establishes that the County acted improperly.

Residents seeking to understand public-policy decisions still face gaps in the available record, including questions about how the Council restructuring began and how concerns raised by residents were handled.

We Recognize That Legitimate Privilege Must Be Protected

To be clear, GAAA is not seeking privileged legal advice.

Legitimate attorney-client communications should remain protected.

The existence of a communication with an attorney does not, by itself, tell us what that communication contained.

Nor does a claim of attorney-client privilege, standing alone, establish that information was improperly withheld.

Our purpose is to document what the County produced, what it withheld, what it reported about its records searches, and what factual questions remain unanswered.

The Questions That Remain

Why did Community Services Director Lindsey Jorstad forward correspondence concerning GAAA's candidate questionnaire and meeting invitation to County Legal on July 9?

What was the purpose of that communication, and what role, if any, did it play in the handling of Commissioner Ku's inquiry?

After Ku's office requested a written response on July 14, what happened to that request?

The County now states that no responsive documents were located and that no meeting or feedback occurred. Are there any additional existing records that would clarify the sequence?

More broadly, what information was available to elected officials as they considered animal welfare concerns raised by residents?

We will keep asking.

About This Article

This article documents GAAA's experience seeking public records and Gwinnett County's responses through September 20, 2026.

The updates incorporate the County's September 8 production of a redacted July 9 email and its September 9 response concerning Commissioner Ku's requested written response.

The newly produced email predates Ku's July 14 preference for a written response. Its body remains redacted, and this article does not claim to know its contents or the reason for contacting County Legal.

A claim of attorney-client privilege does not, by itself, establish improper withholding. The County's report that no responsive documents were found is described as its records-search response.

GAAA will update this article if additional verified records or relevant responses become available.
