A Commissioner Requested the County's Position in Writing. Where Is It?

Gwinnett Animal Advocacy Alliance | September 2026

For more than a month, Gwinnett Animal Advocacy Alliance (GAAA) has been using Georgia's Open Records Act to seek records surrounding the dismantling of the Animal Advisory Council and other animal welfare decisions.

We have encountered records withheld under attorney-client privilege, responses stating that no additional records exist, and questions about how decisions were made that remain unanswered.

One recent records request concerns a written response that Commissioner Ben Ku's office specifically asked county staff to provide.

Did Commissioner Ku receive the written response he requested? If so, where is it?

The July 9 Request for a Written Response

On July 9, Commissioner Ben Ku's office contacted Gwinnett Animal Shelter and Community Services leadership seeking the County's "official position or talking points" regarding animal welfare concerns raised by GAAA.

Community Services Director Lindsey Jorstad offered to meet with the Commissioner.

Ku's office responded:

"I spoke with Commissioner Ku, and he would prefer a written response that he can refer to rather than a meeting."

Pretty clear.

A sitting County Commissioner wanted the County's position in writing.

So GAAA submitted an Open Records Request seeking that written response.

What the County Provided

In response, the County provided the same emails GAAA already possessed.

The County also identified one additional responsive email but withheld it entirely under attorney-client privilege.

The County then stated:

"No additional records exist responsive to your request."

That left an important question unanswered.

Was the withheld email the written response Commissioner Ku requested, or was no such response ever created?

The records provided did not establish which explanation was correct.

Our Follow-Up Request

GAAA followed up seeking basic identifying information about the withheld email, including:

  • Who sent the email?
  • Who received it?
  • When was it sent?
  • What was its subject line?

The County responded:

"The obligation under the Open Records Act is to provide documents. We are not required to answer questions. This request is complete."

So we took the County at its word.

GAAA submitted another Open Records Request specifically seeking existing records or data containing that identifying information.

This was a request for existing records, rather than a request for the County to create new explanations.

This Is Not an Isolated Concern

GAAA has encountered similar responses in other records requests.

When we sought records to determine who initiated the Animal Advisory Council restructuring and when, the County acknowledged that responsive records existed but withheld them under attorney-client privilege.

Another resident independently sought records concerning the restructuring and encountered additional records withheld under attorney-client privilege and attorney work product.

Now, while trying to locate a written County response requested by a sitting Commissioner, we have encountered another record withheld under attorney-client privilege.

When multiple records requests about public-policy decisions repeatedly encounter withheld records, residents are left with important questions about how those decisions were made.

We Recognize That Legitimate Privilege Must Be Protected

To be clear, GAAA is not seeking privileged legal advice.

Legitimate attorney-client communications should remain protected.

We also recognize that identifying a responsive email does not necessarily establish that it contains the written position Commissioner Ku requested.

Our concern is that the records received so far have not resolved the underlying question.

We want to understand what written information was provided to elected officials regarding concerns raised by residents about animal welfare.

The Questions That Remain

Did Commissioner Ku ever receive the written response he requested?

If so, where is it?

If no written response was created, what happened to his request?

And how can Gwinnett residents obtain a clearer understanding of the decisions being made about animal welfare?

We will keep asking.

About This Article

This article describes GAAA's experience seeking public records and the responses received from Gwinnett County as of its September 2026 publication.

Quotations attributed to county officials and staff are reproduced from correspondence described by GAAA.

A claim of attorney-client privilege does not, by itself, establish improper withholding. This article does not assert that the withheld email necessarily contains the requested written position.

GAAA will update this article if additional records or relevant responses are received.
