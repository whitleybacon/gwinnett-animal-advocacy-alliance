Animal Welfare & Accountability · Part 3

“The County Doesn't Have the Money”: A Question of Priorities

Gwinnett Animal Advocacy Alliance

When concerns about animal welfare are raised in Gwinnett County, one argument we repeatedly hear is:

“The county doesn't have the money.”

But a recent investment in the Gwinnett Animal Shelter raises important questions about how resources are prioritized.

A Significant Investment in the Building

Gwinnett County Government recently approved another $5.6 million for renovations to the Gwinnett Animal Shelter , bringing the construction contract to more than $10 million.

The broader renovation includes intake and triage space, isolation and quarantine kennels, bonding areas and other facility improvements.

The additional work addresses aging roof and HVAC systems, ventilation, humidity control and disease containment — along with interior and exterior façade improvements, parking-lot repairs and lighting upgrades.

That is a significant investment in the building.

But a $10+ million renovation raises a much bigger question: where is the same level of investment and urgency in addressing the problems that renovations alone cannot solve?

Questions Beyond the Renovation

Where are the additional cameras residents and advocates have requested for greater transparency and accountability?

Where is the meaningful investment in daily enrichment and improved care for animals already confined in the shelter?

Where is the comprehensive strategy to prevent pet overpopulation and reduce the number of animals entering the shelter in the first place?

Where are the additional resources to strengthen veterinary care, volunteer engagement and foster support?

And what is the plan for stray animals being left in our communities when residents who responsibly contain them cannot get Animal Control to pick them up or accept them?

None of those issues were addressed in the presentation.

Instead, officials spoke about taking a “comprehensive approach” to caring for the county asset and positioning the facility for the future.

What Was Missing From the Discussion?

Not one commissioner asked how this $10 million investment would reduce intake, address pet overpopulation, improve daily animal care, or address the burden being placed on residents and rescues when stray animals are left in the community because the county will not pick them up or accept them.

The item passed unanimously without substantive discussion.

It's About Priorities

So this isn't about choosing between fixing a broken HVAC system and funding spay/neuter.

It's about priorities.

A nicer façade doesn't reduce pet overpopulation.

A repaired parking lot doesn't provide transparency.

And façade improvements and parking-lot repairs don't substitute for policies and programs that improve outcomes for animals.

Gwinnett cannot renovate its way out of pet overpopulation.

Real progress requires investing not only in the building, but in prevention, transparency, animal care and the programs that keep animals from entering the shelter in the first place.

Don't just invest in the building.
Invest in the animals.
